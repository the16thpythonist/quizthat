# Research Notes: Tree Height Physics

## Key Finding
Research published in Nature (2004) found that trees have a theoretical maximum height of **122-130 meters** (400-426 feet) due to physics constraints in water transport.

## The Physics
- Trees transport water from roots to leaves through capillary action and the cohesion-tension theory
- As water evaporates from leaves, negative pressure draws water upward
- Gravity and friction in the transport vessels create the fundamental limit
- Leaves at the top of very tall trees exist in "constant drought"
- The relevant capillary dimensions are air-water interfaces in cell walls (5-10 nm pore diameter)

## Current Record
The world's tallest tree is a 116m coastal redwood in California, which is still 6-14 meters below the theoretical maximum.
